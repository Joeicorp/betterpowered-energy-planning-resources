# Planning connected property upgrades

Whole-property planning reduces the risk of choosing one project without considering what it changes downstream. The useful sequence is evidence-led rather than a fixed equipment bundle.

## 1. Define the property question

Start with the owner's goals, reported conditions, utility history, equipment information, roof timing and the decision currently under review.

## 2. Separate known facts from missing evidence

Document what is supported by records or observation, what remains an assumption and what requires measurement or specialist diagnosis. A preliminary online plan is not an on-site diagnostic audit.

## 3. Map project dependencies

Common dependencies include:

- insulation, air sealing, ducts and future HVAC capacity;
- roof condition and the timing of solar installation;
- electrical-service capacity, EV charging and electrification;
- essential loads, battery capacity and backup duration;
- project scope, current incentive rules and contractor proposals.

## 4. Choose the responsible next provider

BetterPowered can continue the advisory or defined assessment scope, identify additional field evidence, review bids or coordinate a contractor match. Installation and trade-specific diagnosis remain with the appropriately qualified contractor or specialist.

## 5. Re-check the plan as evidence changes

Contractor findings, field measurements, updated bids, programme rules and completed work may change the next sensible action. The plan should preserve those changes instead of treating the first recommendation as permanent.
